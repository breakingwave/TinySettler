# PPO reliability: completed 2026-11-11

Retain **PPO-128 provisionally** for its smaller model. PPO-256 showed neither a repeatable common-budget win advantage nor a persistent reduction in stalled games. This does not establish architectural equivalence: uncertainty still permits meaningful differences. Training and checkpoint-selection stability merit attention before expanding architectures or introducing stronger opponents.

All six fresh runs completed **434,176 transitions** each: 2,605,056 new transitions, with five training seeds per width after including history. Full coverage was retained: 20 validation boards per checkpoint and 50 untouched test boards, each with both seats against Random, WeightedRandom and the existing heuristic. There were 4,800 validation games, 3,000 primary test games and 1,500 additional selected-checkpoint tests. No training run was incomplete; all 9,300 evaluation games had zero illegal actions.

The PPO-128 minus PPO-256 difference is **+0.93 percentage points**. Its 95% board-cluster interval, conditional on these fitted policies, is **[−2.53, +4.40] pp**. Resampling paired training seeds as well as shared board clusters gives **[−5.53, +7.27] pp**. Each board cluster retains both seats and all opponents. These are percentile bootstrap intervals with 10,000 draws; five training seeds remain preliminary evidence.

Common-final win fractions ranged from **67.67–90.00%** for PPO-128 and **70.33–90.00%** for PPO-256. Observed between-seed standard deviations were **8.49 and 8.58 pp**, respectively. PPO-128 won the width comparison in three seed pairs; PPO-256 won two. The compact policy is useful against this benchmark, but consistently high performance across seeds is not established. [Per-seed table](common-step-per-seed.csv) · [Seed comparison plot](common-final-per-seed.png) · [Paired intervals](paired-comparisons.json).

Against Random / WeightedRandom / the heuristic, common-final win fractions were **88.0 / 83.6 / 69.8%** for PPO-128 and **87.8 / 80.2 / 70.6%** for PPO-256. [Full opponent outcomes and points](opponent-comparison.json).

The revised validation procedure changed **three of four historical checkpoint selections**. It uses 120 games and selects by win fraction, then fewer truncations, then earlier checkpoint; the historical procedure used 12 games and mean reward. All selections were hash-committed externally before final testing.

On the secondary endpoint, selected policies achieved **83.40%** wins with **29 truncations** for PPO-128, and **80.33%** with **17 truncations** for PPO-256. The +3.07 pp width difference has a board-only interval of **[+0.07, +6.07]**, but a seed-and-board interval of **[−4.60, +11.47]**. The narrow conditional interval is not evidence of reliable superiority across training seeds. Selection improved pooled win fractions but increased compact-policy stalls; notably, seed 196613 moved from 203 wins / 7 truncations at the final checkpoint to 228 wins / 16 truncations at the selected checkpoint. Investigate selection and stability before claiming overtraining. [Selected-policy table](selected-checkpoint-per-seed.csv) · [Validation curves](learning-curves.png) · [Secondary intervals](selected-paired-comparisons.json).

All **43 primary truncations reached the 1,000-turn limit**, not the 20,000-action cap. They averaged 564 / 578 repeated public-board/own-hand states for the two widths. These repetition counts omit phase and clock and are indicators, not proof of a causal loop. Action-type counts, end-turn frequency, maritime trades and scores are recorded for every new truncation, without hidden opponent cards. [Diagnostics](timeout-diagnostics.json) · [Summary](timeout-summary.json).

New training averaged **19.41 / 19.59 minutes** per run and **373 / 369 learner decisions/s**. The six runs produced 8,829 terminal rewards, about 0.339% of transitions. Model sizes are 321,869 / 676,173 parameters. Measured network/action inference was **0.399 / 0.408 ms inside Colab**, excluding observation construction and host-to-device transfer. Median workload utilization was about one CPU core and 10% GPU, while training time remained similar despite the larger parameter count. [Time accounting](time-accounting.json) · [Utilization](utilization.json) · [Wall-clock curves](wall-clock-curves.png).

The conservative allocation total was **13,054 seconds (3 h 37 m 34 s)** across two sequential T4 sessions, below the authorized five-hour cap. A preflight upload-back RPC timed out and its runtime was stopped; one replacement retained the original deadline. Cached calibration timing was corrected with fresh batches before training. No rules, rewards, opponents, learning settings or transition budgets changed. The four frozen core-file hashes match history; Colab supplied Python 3.13.16 versus historical 3.13.18, which is recorded alongside the unchanged PyTorch 2.9.1 and simulator revision.

All **31 retrieved checkpoints** passed hash, provenance and finite-value checks; all six new final checkpoints passed CPU collection/update recovery, and the external pilot round trip reproduced CUDA continuation and optimizer updates exactly. The 15 common-final/selected policies are packaged with source and manifests. Runtime termination was independently confirmed. [Completion audit](completion-verification.json) · [Recovery checks](recovery-checks.json) · [Checkpoint package and hashes](checkpoint-package-manifest.json) · [Launch/resume/evaluation commands](../../reliability/README.md).

This experiment measures reliability against the specified opponents, **not negotiation quality, general Catan strength or browser latency**. The heuristic ranks every maritime trade below ending the turn. PPO action probabilities are neither Q-values nor calibrated win probabilities. A subsequent study should address training/selection stability, then separately introduce stronger frozen opponents or historical self-play.
